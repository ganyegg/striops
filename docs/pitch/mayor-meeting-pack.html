02The sixty-second answer

If the Mayor asks “what exactly is this?”
Striops is a Strategic Intelligence Operating System. It keeps a continuously-updated, source-linked model of the city — money, water, energy, safety, waste — and runs forecasting, risk and simulation over it to produce a daily executive brief with recommended decisions. Every number carries its source. The AI writes the prose; it never writes the arithmetic. It is not a dashboard, not a consultancy report, and not a chatbot on a data lake.
If the Mayor asks “why would I buy this?”
Because your quarterly reports are true on the day they are printed and stale by the time you act on them. Striops holds the same facts, refreshed continuously, and it remembers why every decision was made. Non-revenue water alone costs the City roughly R185 million a year for every percentage point of system input lost. The pilot costs R180,000. If it changes one decision by a thousandth of a percentage point, it has paid for itself.

Both answers are drawn from docs/STRIOPS.md §0 and §7 and are consistent with the pitch deck.

05Do not say these numbers

The system is currently surfacing four claims that will not survive contact with the relevant director. Each is a data-quality artefact, not a finding. Read this section before you open the app in front of anyone.

1. “Refuse complaints are up 112% in a month”

Refuse service requests ran between roughly 8,400 and 13,500 a month for fifteen straight months, then June 2026 posts 28,604. The same extract also records 336 for September 2025, which is plainly broken. Two impossible months in one series means the extract changed, not the service. The Director of Urban Waste Management will know complaints did not double.

2. “Streetlight faults are up 116% in a month”

Public lighting outages sat between 7,900 and 11,200 a month through April 2026, then May posts 20,154 and June posts 43,588 — a fourfold ramp in two months, in the same two months the refuse series breaks. One shared cause, almost certainly a change in how C3 service requests are categorised or de-duplicated.

3. The app contradicts itself on streetlights

The Wins page currently reads “public lighting outages trending down, faults falling about 2.7% per period — safer streets at night” while the Pulse reads “up 116.3%”. Both are on screen in the same product. If the Mayor clicks between the two pages, the credibility argument is over. This is the single highest-priority fix.

4. Two different dam levels, both labelled live

The City series shows 61.35% for January 2026 and stops there; the DWS weekly series shows 78.2% for August 2026. They are seventeen points apart and both are tagged live, because they measure different things over different periods. Present the DWS figure as the current position, and describe the City series as the City's own historical record — do not show them side by side without that sentence. Note also that the DWS series holds only two months so far, so its “flat, 0.0%” month-on-month reading establishes currency, not a trend.

And handle the underspend figures carefully

The R1.21bn Water and Sanitation underspend, and the R0.88bn on Roads and Transport, come from demonstration full-year function totals, not the City's official mid-year year-to-date position for FY2025/26. The system labels them as such and so must you. The correct framing is method, not amount: “this is the shape of the analysis — wire the S52 tables in the pilot and it becomes your number.”

06Fix before you walk in

  1. Get the live site current. striops-web.onrender.com and striops-api.onrender.com are both serving the build from 22 July. Everything since is on GitHub but never deployed. Consequences: /themes, /pulse, /sectors, /risks, /act, /sources, /simulate, /briefing and /opportunities all return 404 in production, the crime and census feeds are absent, and the feed register shows five sources instead of nine. Open the Render dashboard, check why auto-deploy stopped for both services, and redeploy from main.
  2. Reconcile the streetlight contradiction. Pulse and Wins must not disagree on screen. Either suppress the artefact months from the trend claim or label them as unverified — but not both directions at once.
  3. Guard the refuse and lighting outliers. A point several times outside the trailing range should be flagged for verification rather than headlined as a month-on-month finding.
  4. Run an ingest against production once it is current, so the deployed database holds the Treasury, SAPS, DWS, Census and AGSA series rather than falling back to seed.
  5. Clear the two overdue actions or restate their dates. act-refuse-capacity was due 30 June and act-pipe-programme-report 31 July. An accountability product that shows its own overdue items is either an honesty asset or an unforced error, depending entirely on whether you raise it first.
  6. Have a fallback demo ready. If Render cannot be fixed in time, run the current build locally and present that, rather than a stale public URL.

Turn item 5 into an asset

“Two of the five actions in here are overdue, and the system says so on its own front page. That is the point. A report cannot embarrass you in nine months' time. This can, which is why it changes behaviour.”

08The ask

Do not ask for a budget decision in the room. Ask for the two things that make the next conversation inevitable.

  1. A data-sharing MOU covering aggregates only. No citizen personal information in scope. The specific extracts that unlock the most value: Finance S52 year-to-date operating and capital tables, and Water and Sanitation's official annual system input and zone-level non-revenue water. Those two turn the largest demonstration numbers in this pack into official ones.
  2. A named executive sponsor and a 90-day pilot at R180,000, measured against a written success test agreed up front.
The close
Give me two feeds and ninety days. If at the end of it the daily brief has not changed a single decision, do not renew — and you keep the data, the exports and the schema regardless. There is no proprietary lock on your own facts.

09Objections, and the answers

If they sayYou say
“My team can produce this report.”Once. Striops produces it every morning from live systems, every number able to defend itself, and it never forgets why a decision was made.
“Is this live City data?”Today: curated seed plus cached public feeds — Treasury, SAPS, DWS, Stats SA and AGSA. In the pilot: Finance and Water live. The feed panel in the product always states which, per source.
“AI makes things up.”The AI writes prose only. Scores, forecasts and rand figures are deterministic and assumption-linked. Remove the API key and every number still stands.
“What about POPIA?”Aggregates only. No citizen personal information in scope, and the MOU forbids it.
“Where does it sit?”Pilot in South African region or hybrid; production target inside the City tenancy with single sign-on.
“What if you disappear?”Data in the City tenancy, open exports, continuity clauses, and no proprietary formats holding your facts hostage.
“Your crime data is a year old.”Correct, and the product says so on the row. That is the national release cycle. Wire the City's precinct extract and it becomes monthly — which is precisely what the pilot is for.
“Refuse complaints did not double.”Agreed — that is an extract artefact, and the fact that it is visible is the argument for a provenance layer. Here is the trailing range it broke out of.

How the City requires the meeting to be requested

All meetings are scheduled at the Executive Mayor's discretion, and the published process asks for four things in writing:

  1. A clear statement of your reason for wanting to meet.
  2. A list of all officials you have consulted before requesting the meeting.
  3. A proposed agenda.
  4. A list of proposed attendees and their designations.

Submit by email to mayor.mayor@capetown.gov.za, or hand-deliver into the Mayor's internal post box at the Podium Block information desk, Civic Centre.

Act on point 2 first. An approach that lists Gareth Morgan and Kevin Jacoby as already consulted is materially stronger than a cold request, and it is the cheapest thing in this pack to fix.
